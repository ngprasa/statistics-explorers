Sampling distribution of the sample variance

The sample variance is itself a statistic. Repeated random samples therefore produce a sampling distribution of S².

For a random sample from a normal population,
(n − 1)S² / σ² ~ chi-square with n − 1 degrees of freedom.
This is an exact result, not an approximation.

What is different from the sample mean?

The Central Limit Theorem gives a general result about the sampling distribution of the sample mean: for a very broad class of populations, it becomes approximately Gaussian as n becomes large.

There is no corresponding universal CLT-like result for S² that says its sampling distribution becomes Gaussian simply because n becomes large. For a normal population, the exact sampling distribution is related to chi-square instead. For non-normal populations, the shape depends on the population distribution and there is no single chi-square family that describes it.

Important: this does not mean that S² can never have an approximately normal sampling distribution under particular conditions. It means there is no universal rule analogous to the CLT for the sample mean that students can apply simply from knowing that n is large.

Try this

  1. Choose the Normal population. Compare n = 5, 10, 30 and 50. Watch the lower graph and the chi-square overlay.
  2. Switch between Raw sample variance and Scaled variance. What changes? What stays the same?
  3. For a normal population, increase n. How does the chi-square distribution change as its degrees of freedom increase?
  4. Now choose the Right-skewed population. Increase n. Does the sampling distribution of S² simply become Gaussian in the way the sample mean does?
  5. Repeat with the Uniform and Bimodal populations. Does one universal curve describe all of them?
  6. Keep n fixed and increase the number of repeated samples. What changes: the underlying sampling distribution, or how clearly you see it?

A useful connection

The normal-population result is the bridge to the chi-square distribution. The same theoretical reference is kept visible even when the population is non-normal, so you can see what changes when the normal-population assumption is broken:

Normal population → sample variance → scaled variance → chi-square distribution.

This is the key construction you will need when we move from chi-square to the t distribution.
