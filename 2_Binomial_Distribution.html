Why does the binomial formula look like that?

P(X=r) = C(n,r) pr qn−r
Step 1 — Why pr qn−r?

Suppose exactly r of n trials are successes. A particular sequence with r successes and n−r failures has probability prqn−r.

Step 2 — But there are many possible sequences

For n = 4 and r = 2: HHTT, HTHT, HTTH, THHT, THTH, TTHH. There are 6.

Step 3 — Where does C(n,r) come from?

C(n,r) counts how many ways r successes can be placed among n trials.

Aha: The binomial formula is not a mysterious probability formula. It is probability of one particular sequence × number of sequences that have r successes.

Three questions to investigate

  1. Find values of n and p for which the mean is not an integer. Is the mean itself a possible value of X?
  2. Keep n fixed. Find where the variance is largest. Can you explain why?
  3. Keep p = 0.5 and increase n. Does the distribution get wider, narrower, or simply change scale? Compare the variance and the standard deviation.
