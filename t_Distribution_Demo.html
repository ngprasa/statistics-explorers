Student's t Distribution

Change the degrees of freedom, compare the t distribution with the standard normal, and explore areas under the t curve.
Definition
If Z has a standard normal distribution, V has a chi-square distribution with ν degrees of freedom, and Z and V are independent, then
T = Z / √(V / ν)
has a Student's t distribution with ν degrees of freedom.
This representation shows t as a standard-normal quantity divided by a random estimate of scale. It is the bridge to the familiar t-statistic formula used later.

The standard normal curve is fixed. As the degrees of freedom increase, the t distribution approaches it. At low df, the t distribution has a lower centre and heavier tails.
Mean and variance of a t distribution

Mean: 0 when ν > 1; undefined when ν ≤ 1.
Variance: ν / (ν − 2) when ν > 2.
For 1 < ν ≤ 2, the variance is infinite. For ν ≤ 1, it is undefined.
Things to notice
  1. Set df = 1, 2, 5, 10, 30 and 100. Watch how the tails change relative to the standard normal.
  2. At low df, compare the height near 0 with the standard normal. Where has the extra probability gone?
  3. Keep the selected t value fixed and increase df. How does the tail probability change?
  4. Try the central-area option. For the same |t|, compare the central probability for low and high df.
  5. At large df, explain why the t distribution begins to behave almost like Z.
